CLARIS was our attempt at restoring clear voice for people suffering from speech impairments. For the project to actually be useful, it must be present with users whenever they need, making the mobile phones the most obvious place to deploy the model. However, we cannot rely on people to have high-end phones with flagship SoCs, meaning the optimizing the model inference must be fast without sacrificing output quality.

In this article, we walk through the engineering journey of taking an auto-regressive Speech-to-Unit (S2U) Transformer model from PyTorch training code to a real-time inference pipeline using **ONNX Runtime** and **LiteRT**.

## 1. System Architecture & Mathematical Formulation

The CLARIS framework operates on a **Speech-to-Unit (S2U)** paradigm: rather than directly generating continuous spectral features (which often suffer from over-smoothing or acoustic artifacts when input speech is severely degraded), it predicts a sequence of discrete acoustic units corresponding to clean speech.

$$
\underbrace{X}_{\substack{\text{Atypical} \\ \text{Mel-Spectrogram}}} \xrightarrow{\text{Encoder}} \mathbf{H}_X \xrightarrow[\text{Beam Search}]{\substack{\text{AR Decoder} \\ + \ \mathbf{e}_s}} \underbrace{u_{1:N}}_{\substack{\text{Discrete Units} \\ (\text{mHuBERT-147})}} \xrightarrow{\substack{\text{Code HiFi-GAN} \\ + \ \mathbf{e}_s}} \underbrace{\hat{y}}_{\substack{\text{Restored} \\ \text{Waveform}}}
$$

The end-to-end pipeline consists of three core stages:
1. **Acoustic Encoder**: Processes the atypical input mel-spectrogram $X$ into a continuous latent representation $\mathbf{H}_X$.
2. **Auto-Regressive Decoder**: Generates discrete acoustic units $u_{1:N}$ from the mHuBERT-147 codebook conditioned on $\mathbf{H}_X$ and target speaker timbre embedding $\mathbf{e}_s$ using Beam Search.
3. **Neural Vocoder**: Synthesizes the final high-fidelity time-domain audio waveform $\hat{y}$ from discrete tokens using a Code HiFi-GAN model.

### Discrete Acoustic Units via mHuBERT-147

To represent clean target speech as discrete tokens, we use a multilingual **mHuBERT-147** self-supervised model. Continuous hidden representations from an intermediate transformer layer are clustered via $k$-means into a discrete codebook $\mathcal{C} = \{1, 2, \dots, K\}$:

$$
u_t = \text{k-means}\Big(f_{\text{mHuBERT}}(Y)_t\Big) \in \{1, \dots, K\}
$$

These discrete units discard speaker-specific noise while retaining rich phonetic and linguistic information.

### Auto-Regressive Formulation

Given the input mel-spectrogram $X \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times F}$ of the atypical speech, the encoder produces representations $\mathbf{H}_X = \text{Encoder}(X)$. The auto-regressive decoder then models the probability distribution of clean discrete unit sequence $u_{1:N}$ conditioned on $\mathbf{H}_X$ and target speaker timbre embedding $\mathbf{e}_s$:

$$
P(u_{1:N} \mid X, \mathbf{e}_s) = \prod_{t=1}^N P(u_t \mid u_{<t}, \mathbf{H}_X, \mathbf{e}_s)
$$

### Beam Search Decoding & Speech Synthesis

During inference, to prevent degenerative loops and capture globally optimal phonetic trajectories, CLARIS uses **Beam Search** with width $B$ to decode the unit sequence:

$$
\hat{u}_{1:N} = \arg\max_{u_{1:N}} \sum_{t=1}^N \log P(u_t \mid u_{<t}, \mathbf{H}_X, \mathbf{e}_s)
$$

The decoded unit sequence $\hat{u}_{1:N}$ is then mapped through a learned unit-embedding lookup and synthesized into the final time-domain audio waveform $\hat{y}$ using a **Code HiFi-GAN** neural vocoder conditioned on speaker timbre $\mathbf{e}_s$:

$$
\hat{y} = \text{Vocoder}(\hat{u}_{1:N}, \mathbf{e}_s)
$$

## 3. Exporting Dynamic-Axis Models with KV-Cache

To achieve cross-platform execution on mobile and embedded devices, we export the PyTorch model to the **ONNX (Open Neural Network Exchange)** format. 

Rather than exporting the entire auto-regressive generation loop as a single monolithic ONNX graph, we **decouple the encoder and decoder** into separate models:

1. **Acoustic Encoder (`encoder.onnx`)**: Evaluated exactly once per utterance over the variable-length mel-spectrogram input.
2. **Single-Step Decoder (`decoder.onnx`)**: Evaluated iteratively at each auto-regressive generation step $t$, receiving the single latest token and the Key-Value (KV) cache from previous steps.

By keeping the beam search and token selection logic entirely in native **C++**, we gain complete control over memory management, candidate pruning, and thread pooling without needing to re-export or modify the ONNX computation graphs.

The export script is available in our inference repository: [`CLARIS-Inference/export.py`](https://github.com/Yash-Kumar-tech/CLARIS-Inference/blob/main/export.py).

### Why Explicit KV-Caching is Essential

In naive auto-regressive generation, passing the entire sequence of generated tokens $u_{1:t}$ at step $t$ forces the self-attention layers to recompute keys and values for all past tokens:

$$
\text{Attention}(\mathbf{Q}_t, \mathbf{K}_{1:t}, \mathbf{V}_{1:t}) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}_t \mathbf{K}_{1:t}^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) \mathbf{V}_{1:t}
$$

Recomputing $\mathbf{K}_{1:t}$ and $\mathbf{V}_{1:t}$ at every step leads to an algorithmic time complexity of $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ for generating a sequence of length $N$.

By maintaining a **KV-cache** tensor between successive calls, the decoder only projects the query $\mathbf{Q}_t$ for the latest incoming token, concatenating the new key-value pair $(\mathbf{k}_t, \mathbf{v}_t)$ onto the pre-existing cache:

$$
\mathbf{K}_{\text{present}} = [\mathbf{K}_{\text{past}} \,;\, \mathbf{k}_t], \quad \mathbf{V}_{\text{present}} = [\mathbf{V}_{\text{past}} \,;\, \mathbf{v}_t]
$$

This reduces per-step computation to $\mathcal{O}(1)$, achieving $\mathcal{O}(N)$ linear decoding time over the entire sequence.

### Specifying Dynamic Axes for ONNX Export

Because audio recordings and generated unit sequences naturally vary in length, all sequence dimensions must be explicitly marked as dynamic in `torch.onnx.export`:

```python
import torch

# 1. Export Acoustic Encoder (Runs once per utterance)
torch.onnx.export(
    encoder_model,
    args=(dummy_mel_input,),
    f="encoder.onnx",
    input_names=["mel_spec"],
    output_names=["encoder_hidden"],
    dynamic_axes={
        "mel_spec": {0: "batch_size", 1: "num_mels", 2: "time_frames"},
        "encoder_hidden": {0: "batch_size", 1: "time_frames", 2: "hidden_dim"},
    },
    opset_version=17,
)

# 2. Export Single-Step Decoder with KV-Cache (Evaluated per auto-regressive step)
torch.onnx.export(
    decoder_wrapper,
    args=(dummy_token_id, dummy_encoder_hidden, dummy_past_kv),
    f="decoder_with_cache.onnx",
    input_names=["input_id", "encoder_hidden", "past_key_values"],
    output_names=["logits", "present_key_values"],
    dynamic_axes={
        "input_id": {0: "batch_size"},
        "encoder_hidden": {0: "batch_size", 1: "enc_time_frames"},
        "past_key_values": {2: "batch_size", 4: "past_seq_len"},
        "logits": {0: "batch_size"},
        "present_key_values": {2: "batch_size", 4: "present_seq_len"},
    },
    opset_version=17,
)
```

## 4. Conclusion

Deploying deep generative speech models on resource-constrained edge devices requires careful co-design between the neural architecture and the inference runtime. By combining discrete acoustic units, explicit KV-caching in ONNX, and OpenVINO INT8 kernel fusion, real-time assistive speech restoration is now feasible on standard consumer hardware.
